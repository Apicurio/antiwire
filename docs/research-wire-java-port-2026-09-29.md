# Research Report: Porting Square Wire to Pure Java (Project "antiwire")

**Date**: 2026-09-29, revised the same day after the external plan review
**Depth**: exhaustive
**Confidence**: HIGH for dependency/footprint/test-infrastructure facts (verified against the real artifacts: cloned sources, published POMs, jar sizes); MEDIUM for effort estimates and performance expectations. Feasibility remains unproven until the M0 spikes run; no code exists yet.

Approved decisions are recorded in [decisions.md](decisions.md); the disposition of every review finding is recorded in [plan-review.md](plan-review.md). This report carries the evidence and reasoning.

## Executive Summary

Wire 7.1.0 (released 2026-09-28; annotated tag object `da24c33ee1fe772a7a04617018087f46f26d1708` pointing at commit `9f62097dfe4995b5709d001ca0187e30ca0530ef`) is a Kotlin Multiplatform project. A clean consumer of its `wire-schema` artifact resolves kotlin-stdlib (1.7 MB), okio-jvm (0.4 MB, itself Kotlin), kotlinpoet and guava; `wire-runtime` alone pulls only kotlin-stdlib and okio (F2). Apicurio Registry consumes exactly one Wire artifact, `wire-schema`, for schema parsing and the `Schema` model; Kafka today has no Wire dependency at all, and trunk practice suggests, though the review found no primary written Kafka policy confirming or refuting it, that the Kotlin chain would not be accepted there. The port surface is well-bounded: about 8.2k LOC for `wire-runtime` (JVM slice) plus about 11.8k LOC for `wire-schema` (JVM slice), with no coroutines and no value classes in that surface, and unsigned usage confined to two parse sites with direct Java mappings. The same-test-suite requirement is achievable under the adapted-test model: Wire's tests are Kotlin but run on the JVM, so they stay Kotlin in the port's test scope with mechanical, individually reviewed adaptations, because Kotlin cannot call Java declarations with named arguments, while Java overload bridges can support some omitted-default calls and hand-written `copy()`/`componentN()` bridges remain possible from Java. The external oracle is Wire's `wire-protoc-compatibility-tests` suite, which validates against protoc 4.36.1 output. The okio entanglement (ByteString and BufferedSource in the runtime API, plus `FileSystem` and `Path` across wire-schema's public surface) is resolved by an M0 spike comparing a vendored subset, scoped java.nio, and reviewed pure-Java dependencies over a full inventory; no route is preselected. Estimated 4 to 6 months of focused work through six milestones, provisional until M0 measures the I/O subset scope and translation velocity; Apicurio integration is planned after the schema milestones and is not claimed as proven.

### F2. Dependency footprint (HIGH, verified against published POMs)

Published POMs on Maven Central, wire 7.1.0:

- `wire-runtime-jvm` dependencies: **okio-jvm, kotlin-stdlib**. Jar size 0.3 MB.
- `wire-schema-jvm` dependencies: **okio-jvm, guava, javapoet (com.palantir), kotlinpoet-jvm, wire-runtime-jvm, kotlin-stdlib**. Jar size 0.5 MB.

Reference jar sizes, measured 2026-09-29 as published Content-Length: wire-runtime-jvm 7.1.0 is 283,191 bytes; wire-schema-jvm 7.1.0 is 514,450 bytes; kotlin-stdlib 2.0.21 is 1,747,660 bytes; okio-jvm 3.18.2 is 392,462 bytes; guava 33.7.1-jre is 3,057,607 bytes (2.92 MiB); protobuf-java 3.25.5 is 1.8 MB. javapoet and kotlinpoet-jvm sizes were not measured.

The guava dependency is nearly free to remove: inside wire-schema only `Multimap.kt` touches `com.google.common` (2 imports). The kotlinpoet/javapoet `api` dependencies exist because `AdapterConstant.kt` and `Profile.kt` reference poet types; these are code-generation concerns, not schema-model concerns, and the approved plan moves that support into the optional generator artifact.

Footprint must be stated as two different numbers, both measured rather than estimated, and both counting the poet jars, since the wire-schema POM makes kotlinpoet-jvm and javapoet mandatory alongside guava. Of the marginal chain (a consumer that already has guava), the measured subset is wire-schema-jvm 514,450 + wire-runtime-jvm 283,191 + okio-jvm 392,462 + kotlin-stdlib 1,747,660 bytes, which sums to 2,937,763 bytes (2.94 MB decimal, 2.80 MiB) before the unmeasured kotlinpoet-jvm and javapoet jars. The clean consumer adds guava at 3,057,607 bytes, giving a measured subset of 5,995,370 bytes (6.0 MB decimal, 5.72 MiB), again excluding the unmeasured poets. Complete totals for either case are deliberately not quoted: they stay unmeasured until TASK-19 resolves the full graph against each consumer's pre-existing dependencies. No port artifact size or savings figure is promised here. Under the approved dependency policy (production artifacts contain no Kotlin, reviewed pure-Java dependencies allowed), the port's footprint is a measurement outcome of TASK-19, taken from resolved dependency trees on a clean minimal consumer and on the Apicurio integration branch, with the optional generator's cost reported separately.

### F3. What "pure Java" must actually replace (HIGH for facts, from source audit)

- **okio is embedded in the public API, well beyond ByteString.** `AnyMessage.value: ByteString`, `ProtoAdapter.BYTES`, and every read/write path use `BufferedSource`/`BufferedSink`; the import scan at the tag also finds `okio.IOException`, `okio.EOFException`, `okio.Closeable`, `okio.utf8Size`, ByteString companion operations (`decodeBase64`, `decodeHex`, `encodeUtf8`, `toByteString`), `Path.Companion.toPath`, `okio.sink/source/buffer`, `okio.openZip`, `okio.asResourceFileSystem`, and `FileSystem.Companion.asOkioFileSystem`; and `okio.FileSystem`/`okio.Path` appear in the wire-schema public API across 14 main files (SchemaLoader, Schema, Root, WireRun, SchemaHandler, ClaimedPaths, CoreLoader, Roots, WireLoggers, the parser, Util). Tests bind `FakeFileSystem` and `"path".toPath()`, and the loader's compression-relevant concern is ZIP, not gzip. Depending on okio-jvm defeats the purpose (it is Kotlin), so the I/O route is an M0-owned spike decision ([decisions.md](decisions.md) OPEN-1) comparing reviewed pure-Java dependencies, a translated okio subset under original or relocated names, a scoped java.nio replacement, or a bounded combination, against this full inventory. okio 1.x (line ended at 1.17.6) is a vendoring precedent, but its source purity is unverified and it lacks Path/FileSystem entirely, so it is not assumed to be a drop-in replacement.
- **wire-schema's JVM slice is tiny** (832 LOC) and its only heavy parts are poet references (moved to the optional generator artifact under the approved plan) and guava (trivially replaceable).
- **No hard Kotlin-language blockers in the port surface**: no `suspend`, no value classes in runtime/schema main sources (the `value class` grep hits are annotation definition files), and unsigned usage is confined to two parse intermediates in `SchemaEncoder.kt` (`String.toUInt().toInt()` / `toULong().toLong()`), mapping directly to `Integer.parseUnsignedInt` / `Long.parseUnsignedLong`. Sealed oneof types are markers (`WireSealedOneof`) that map to plain Java hierarchies; a Java 11 baseline forbids `sealed` anyway, while local-variable `var` remains available.
- **Semantics to watch**: Kotlin `require` throws IllegalArgumentException (same as Java), but `checkNotNull` throws **IllegalStateException**, not NullPointerException; tests assert exception types, so translation conventions must preserve them. Data-class equals/hashCode/toString and `List` equality semantics must be reproduced exactly. Kotlin callers of Java classes cannot use named arguments, and Java declarations have no default parameters, so adapted upstream tests rewrite named-argument call forms and handle omitted-default call sites individually, since Java overload bridges can support some of them; hand-written `copy()`/`componentN()` bridges and overload bridges for defaults remain possible from Java and are the convention where tests need them.
- **Provenance**: five upstream files carry BSD-style notices, retained verbatim: `ProtoReader.kt`, `ProtoReader32.kt`, `ByteArrayProtoReader32.kt`, `internal/MathMethods.kt` (R8), and `ProtoWriter.kt`, whose Square Apache-2.0 header (lines 1 to 15) is followed by a Google Nano BSD-style notice embedded in the file body (lines 18 to 50); `internal/*ArrayList.kt` carry JetBrains Apache-2.0 headers. The port is a derivative work and must preserve all attribution, scanning file bodies and not only leading headers; NOTICE carries the BSD-style notices verbatim plus separate Apache-2.0 attribution for any vendored okio code ([decisions.md](decisions.md) DEC-12). Wire is Apache 2.0 overall; legal approval is not claimed.

### F4. Test suites: what "pass the same test suite" means concretely (HIGH)

Wire has no Google-conformance-suite harness (repo-wide grep found none). Its verification stack is:

1. **Internal unit suites** (multiplatform, JVM-runnable). Within the wire-runtime module there is **no jvmTest source set at all**; that module's suite is commonTest only. Applicable runtime behavior tests are not confined to that module: the shared `wire-tests` module carries executable JVM suites (including `jvm-java-kotlin`) with cases such as `UnknownFieldsTest` (encode/decode with unknown-field retention and equality/hashCode assertions, `@Test` methods from line 35 onward), `SerializableTest`, and `RuntimeMessageAdapterRedactTest`, verified by direct read at the pinned commit; these are runtime coverage, not fixture code, and belong to the port's mandatory scope. wire-schema has both commonTest and jvmTest. Frameworks: kotlin.test, assertk, junit 4; the schema suites also use okio-fakefilesystem, jimfs, protobuf-java, wire-test-utils, wire-schema-tests. Counting method and scope: count `@Test`-annotated functions per source set at the pinned tag. Measured so far (single recount, to be re-derived mechanically by the M0 harness extraction): runtime commonTest 13 files / 65 tests; schema commonTest 21 files / 274; schema jvmTest 12 files / 278. These figures are counts for those named source sets only, not complete runtime-scope counts, since the wire-tests behavior suites are not yet counted; no full count is asserted. This report's original approximate figures were higher; the M0 runner's mechanical count is authoritative.
2. **External oracle**: `wire-protoc-compatibility-tests` (8 test files / 122 tests by the same recount; Proto2/Proto3 interop, roundtrips through protoc-generated code) built with the google protobuf Gradle plugin pinned to **protoc 4.36.1**, sourcing `descriptor.proto` from the protobuf-java jar. Its dev-time dependencies include gson-support, moshi-adapter, grpc-client, and Kotlin-generator fixture modes, so it needs per-file applicability triage rather than wholesale adoption.
3. **Golden files**: `wire-golden-files` corpus of generated code, regenerated through WireCompiler. The Java portion is exactly one file (`AllTypes.java`), so golden identity alone is thin evidence; the plan adds Java-target compiler tests to compensate.
4. Adapter suites (gson, moshi), the gRPC reflection product `wire-reflector` and its grpcurl interop suite, gradle-plugin tests: out of scope under the declared exclusions. This excludes the gRPC reflection product only; the runtime's JVM reflection machinery is a different thing and its tests are mandatory.

Strategy that satisfies the requirement under the approved test scope ([decisions.md](decisions.md) DEC-5): **upstream Kotlin test sources stay Kotlin in the port's test scope** (Kotlin allowed for build and test only), run against the Java production artifacts with mechanical, individually recorded adaptations, because Kotlin cannot call Java declarations with named arguments (some omitted-default call sites are instead served by Java overload bridges), and upstream tests and generated fixtures use those forms plus package-level internal imports. Pinned originals are preserved for drift comparison; every adaptation maps original to adapted case and preserves inputs, scenarios, expected values, and error paths; only cases exclusively covering declared non-ported functionality are excluded, and mixed suites contribute their Java-target cases. Recent security and semantics changes must be covered since the tests encode them: negative-length group skipping (GHSA-7xpr-hc2w-34m9, fixed 6.3.0), oversized-length and reader-limit rejection (GHSA-9rm7-3qhh-h2mc, fixed 6.4.5 and 7.0.0-alpha04; both advisories are the JVM-relevant pair of the five 2026 GHSAs, the other three being Swift-only), and the duplicate singular message merge (#3652/#3656), which landed in **7.0.0-alpha05** (2026-07-15), not 6.4.x as this report first stated; that same alpha adds FieldMask support. The 7.1.0 JSON null-element rejection fix lives in the excluded gson/moshi adapters and is recorded as out of scope rather than promised.

### F5. Apicurio Registry consumption surface (HIGH for the audited commit; an observed minimum)

Apicurio pins `<wire-schema.version>6.4.0</wire-schema.version>` and depends on exactly `com.squareup.wire:wire-schema` plus `wire-schema-jvm`; nothing else from Wire. Evidence base: a local Apicurio clone at commit `48e2742acc68fae456fe252cc8b08243e376ebfc` (cloned 2026-09-24) plus public fetches of 2026-09-29; the census below is an observed minimum at that state, not a guaranteed-exhaustive or current-master inventory, and the integration task re-inventories before editing. Used API, from imports:

- `com.squareup.wire.schema.internal.parser.*`: ProtoParser, ProtoFileElement, MessageElement, EnumElement/EnumConstantElement, FieldElement, OneOfElement, ReservedElement, RpcElement, ServiceElement, TypeElement (the parser element model; Kotlin `internal` is public in JVM bytecode, and the Java port must keep these public). Apicurio also calls `ProtoParser.Companion.parse(...)` and constructs `ProtoParser` directly.
- `com.squareup.wire.schema.*`: Schema, SchemaException, Location, ProtoFile, Type, Field, Service, **and SchemaLoader**, whose public constructor takes an `okio.FileSystem` at both the 6.4.0 and 7.1.0 tags. The census also imports exactly one wire-runtime type, `com.squareup.wire.Syntax` (`ProtobufFile.java:10`, `FileDescriptorUtils.java:10`), so the runtime keep-name surface covers it.
- **Kotlin and okio types cross the integration boundary**, which is why limited Apicurio source migration is approved project scope ([decisions.md](decisions.md) DEC-2): `ProtobufSchemaLoader.java` imports `okio.FileHandle`, `okio.FileSystem`, and `okio.fakefilesystem.FakeFileSystem` and builds an in-memory filesystem; `ProtoContent.java` imports three okio types; `FileDescriptorUtils.java` and `ProtobufFile.java` import `kotlin.ranges.IntRange` and test parser results with `instanceof`; and the `utils/protobuf-schema-utilities` POM declares `okio-jvm` and `okio-fakefilesystem`. That set (four Java files plus the module POM) is the observed migration minimum. The reserved/extension range branches in `FileDescriptorUtils` end in explicit `IllegalStateException` throws on unsupported types, so a wrong range type fails loudly there; the one silent path is `ProtobufFile.processMessageElement`'s final else-branch, which adds an unrecognized object as a single bogus reserved-field entry, and the delta-aware checks cover it.

Consuming modules: `utils/protobuf-schema-utilities` (ProtobufFile, ProtobufSchema, ProtobufSchemaLoader, FileDescriptorUtils: converts Wire model to Google DescriptorProtos), `schema-util/protobuf` (canonicalizer, dereferencer, validators, compatibility checker, FQN conflict detector, structured content extractor, reference finder), `schema-validation/protobuf` (ProtobufValidator, ProtobufSchemaParser), `serdes/generic/serde-common-protobuf` (schema parser for generic serde), `app` (ccompat v7; plus a tag-extractor file importing parser elements and Location), and two integration-test sources importing `ProtoFileElement` (test scope); 23 files import Wire types in total at the audited commit. Note this is a **schema-model** integration, not a wire-runtime message integration: Apicurio never calls `Schema.protoAdapter`, SchemaEncoder, or any runtime encode/decode path, so the port's schema milestones are what unlock Apicurio, and wire-runtime is in its tree only as wire-schema's dependency. Java 11 bytecode fits Apicurio's build, which compiles protobuf-schema-utilities to release 8 for one execution and as a multi-release jar otherwise.

### F6. Kafka constraints (HIGH for facts; adoption path inherently community-gated)

Kafka trunk (`apache/kafka`, 5.0 line), verified 2026-09-29: `minClientJavaVersion = 11`, `minNonClientJavaVersion = 17` (`build.gradle`). The only protobuf dependency is `com.google.protobuf:protobuf-java:3.25.5`, noted as "a dependency of opentelemetryProto"; there is no Kotlin in the dependency set and no Wire usage (code search returns none). Implications: (a) a library acceptable across Kafka client modules must build at Java 11 bytecode; (b) the current Wire dependency chain (kotlin-stdlib, kotlinpoet, okio, guava) is unlikely to be accepted upstream, an adoption inference drawn from trunk practice; the review found no primary written policy supporting or refuting it, so no established written ban or permission is claimed; (c) actual adoption in Apache Kafka goes through the community (KIP), so the port's job is to make the artifact KIPPable (Apache 2.0, clean provenance, no Kotlin in production, measured footprint, Java 11). Kafka is deferred and does not gate the first release ([decisions.md](decisions.md) DEC-7).

### F7. Prior art (MEDIUM; absence is hard to prove)

No existing pure-Java port or fork of Wire was found via web search and GitHub repository search (queries for wire/protobuf/pure-java ports). The pure-Java ancestor is Wire 2.x itself (last pure-Java release 2.3.0), which is too old to forward-port (proto3 support and the entire current schema/linker architecture postdate it) but is useful precedent that the runtime encoding layer was Java once. Adjacent pure-Java protobuf libraries (protobuf-javalite, protostuff, protobuf-dynamic) solve different problems and do not provide Wire's schema-model + linker, which is the part Apicurio depends on.

### F8. Performance (LOW as a prediction; the policy is settled)

`wire-benchmarks` is a JMH module (AllTypesBenchmark, SimpleMessageBenchmark) usable as the port's runtime comparison harness. The port translates the same algorithms (hand-rolled varint loops; the newer ProtoReader32/ByteArrayProtoReader32 fast paths), so parity is the expectation, but this is unverified and algorithm similarity is not evidence. The approved policy is measure-and-approve ([decisions.md](decisions.md) DEC-9): measured runtime benchmarks against upstream Wire 7.1.0 and measured Apicurio schema-operation benchmarks (parsing, linking, loading, descriptor conversion) are required before the first release; every regression needs explicit maintainer acceptance; numeric thresholds are set only after baseline and variance measurement, never invented in advance.

## Project Plan Analysis

### Goals and non-goals

Goals: (1) pure-Java (Java 11 bytecode) Wire artifacts whose production scope and transitive dependencies contain no Kotlin, with reviewed pure-Java dependencies allowed and their footprint measured; (2) run every applicable upstream test case (runtime including its JVM reflection machinery, core schema, applicable protoc-oracle cases, Java-target compiler cases, applicable security corpus) with tracked mechanical adaptations, at the pinned tag; (3) integrate into Apicurio Registry through limited, documented source migration with delta-aware parity: record upstream 6.4.0 versus 7.1.0 differences first, then require the port to match upstream 7.1.0; (4) become KIPPable for Kafka, a deferred, non-gating goal. Non-goals (initial release): Kotlin/Swift codegen products, the gRPC client and the gRPC reflection product `wire-reflector` with its grpcurl interop suite, moshi and gson adapters, Gradle plugin, editions support (upstream lacks it; diverging is out), JS/native/wasm targets, and any precompiled-ABI promise for Wire 6/7-generated classes.

### Architecture decisions (recorded in [decisions.md](decisions.md))

- **Keep `com.squareup.wire.*` public names where feasible.** The benefit is upstream-test reuse and a smaller integration diff, not binary compatibility: no precompiled Wire 6/7 ABI is promised, and exceptions plus duplicate-class coexistence rules are recorded in the TASK-1 compatibility matrix. Publish under a new Maven groupId, intended `io.apicurio`, conditional on organization authorization not recorded here or assumed.
- **Java 11 bytecode, build on JDK 17+** (`--release 11`). Covers Kafka client modules and Apicurio alike; forbids records and sealed classes in main sources; `var` remains available.
- **I/O route decided by the M0 spike**, not preselected: reviewed pure-Java dependencies, translated okio subset (original or relocated names), scoped java.nio, or a bounded combination, over the full F3 inventory. Whatever the route, reused or translated classes carry okio's behavior tests (adapted) or equivalent demonstrations, and any vendored code keeps its notices with separate attribution.
- **Poet out of the core artifact**: wire-schema ships without kotlinpoet; Java-profile/AdapterConstant support moves to the optional generator module (which may use a reviewed pure-Java JavaPoet), whose tests are owned by the generator task so no required case is lost.
- **Build tool: Maven**, matching Apicurio's consumption model; Kotlin appears only in test compilation and build-time fixture tooling.
- **Parity runner as a first-class instrument**, created early (M0) and completed as the blocking gate (M2): re-extracts pinned upstream sources, preserves originals, applies reviewed adaptation mappings, runs implemented suites against the port, diffs goldens, runs the protoc oracle, and keeps a case inventory with executed, excluded-feature, and required-pending states, where pending never counts as passed.

Milestone IDs match the backlog (the active m-6 through m-11, corresponding to phases M0 through M5; the original m-0 through m-5 files are archived); task references are the backlog IDs.

TASK-19 measures footprint (clean-consumer versus marginal, real coordinates, generator separated). Gate: migrated protobuf and serde tests green, delta documented and accepted, measured footprint accepted.

TASK-23 demonstrates the upstream sync and security-response procedure on an already-published change that includes at least one security fix, rehearsing detection, applicability triage, owner routing with simulated escalation, source diff, adaptation, regression detection, and a full parity rerun locally, with no real incident notifications. TASK-21 releases 0.1.0 gated on TASK-19, TASK-20, and TASK-23, whose closure covers the execution tasks; the release-time checks include the no-Kotlin dependency recheck and the corrected NOTICE inventory. TASK-22, the Kafka scenario approval and POC, follows the release and does not gate it.

Total: roughly **4 to 6 months** of focused single-engineer effort, verification-dominated; a provisional MEDIUM-confidence estimate re-examined at M0 against measured I/O scope, trial translation velocity, and mechanically recounted suite sizes. Translation is heavily automatable; the schedule drivers are the parity runner and the edge-case suites.

### Risk register

1. **I/O route and okio semantics** (route undecided until M0; Buffer segment pooling, ByteString interning/utf8): mitigated by running okio's own behavior tests with reviewed adaptations for reused or translated classes, or equivalent demonstrations for alternatives; residual risk MEDIUM.
2. **Exception-type, nullability, and equality semantics drift** versus Kotlin originals: conventions plus adapted upstream tests with preserved assertions keep it visible; MEDIUM.
3. **Test-adaptation drift** (adaptations quietly weakening scenarios): mitigated by pinned originals, a reviewed mapping ledger covering inputs, helpers, expected values, and error paths, and CI drift checks; MEDIUM.
4. **Upstream drift**: Wire shipped five 2026 GHSAs (two JVM-relevant) plus JVM-relevant fixes carrying no advisory id; the sync procedure is demonstrated before the first release and monitors the changelog, not advisories alone; process risk, MEDIUM.
5. **Editions gap**: both Wire and the port reject editions at the pinned tag; proto2/proto3 scope must be stated loudly to consumers, and the sync policy watches the existing upstream hook; LOW for parity, HIGH as a market fact.
